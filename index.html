Research & industry

Applied research alongside day-to-day operations

Sustainable Lithium Cells Australia operates as both a commercial battery recycling and second-life business and as an active contributor to Australian battery circular economy research and policy development. Our operational experience — dismantling, testing and repurposing batteries since 2021 — informs the research and submissions we contribute to the sector.

🔬

Second-life battery systems research

Grant-funded research through TAFE Queensland's Centre of Excellence — Clean Energy Batteries, developing second-life lithium battery systems for mobile solar applications.

📋

Product stewardship & regulation

Ongoing research into Australian product stewardship frameworks, including the NSW Product Lifecycle Responsibility Act 2025 and associated regulation, and their implications for battery collection and recycling markets.

🚚

Dangerous goods transport

Tracking developments in the Australian Dangerous Goods Code as they apply to end-of-life lithium battery collection and transport, including draft special provisions under consultation.

📊

Collection economics & data

Analysis of lithium battery collection rates, disposal costs and material recovery economics in Australia — including the gap between waste generated and waste recovered.

Industry engagement

We engage with the Australian battery recycling and stewardship sector through industry bodies, conferences and working groups, including the Association for the Battery Recycling Industry (ABRI) and the Battery Stewardship Council / B-Cycle ecosystem. We maintain working relationships with Australian recycling and processing operators including Ecocycle, Envirostream and Renewable Metals.

For research enquiries, data requests or policy correspondence, contact research@2lbatteries.com.au.

Our mission

Closing the gap in the lithium circular economy

Lithium batteries are finite if treated as single-use. We extract and refurbish cells from discarded batteries, giving them a second life — reducing demand for new mining and making renewable energy more affordable.
